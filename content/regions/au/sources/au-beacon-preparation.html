Land-trip beacon preparation: registration, testing and distress plan

Prepare a 406 MHz personal locator beacon (PLB) for an Australian land journey before you need it. This original checklist organises factual points from AMSA's beacon pages; it is not their manual or a device-specific operating procedure. Keep the actual manufacturer's instructions available offline and learn the controls before departure. This page does not establish marine/aviation carriage compliance or overseas permission.

Before the journey: make a device record

  • Manufacturer, model, Hex ID/UIN, owner and where the beacon will be carried:
  • Battery expiry printed on the unit, next service action and the manufacturer's servicing contact:
  • Manufacturer's self-test procedure and permitted frequency; last result and next required action:
  • Where the model's activation, antenna/deployment and indicator instructions are kept:
  • Who can reach and operate it, including if the usual carrier is injured or separated:

AMSA says to follow the manufacturer's self-test instructions and frequency; excessive tests consume battery capacity. Use the intended self-test, not a live distress activation for practice. A distress-used beacon needs advice from its manufacturer or a certified service agent about battery replacement and servicing before relying on it again.

Registration and trip information

AMSA's beacon registrations stopped expiring on 4 November 2024. That change does not extend the battery's expiry date or remove the need to update details. Check the registered identifier, owner and emergency contacts. Keep registration confirmation available offline.

Registration information helps rescuers identify the beacon and contact someone who knows the journey. Record when the trip and contact details were last checked. For a borrowed unit, give the owner your current emergency contacts and have the owner update the account's trip details for the borrowing period. This checklist does not submit registration or trip details.

Preparation record: registration checked on ___; trip details updated by ___; emergency contact has current route version ___; offline confirmation kept at ___; battery expiry ___; unresolved servicing question ___.

Agree on the distress plan

Know which phone or radio could obtain emergency help and exchange information. AMSA advises beacon activation for life-threatening distress when two-way communication is unavailable. Follow the activation instructions for the actual model. Do not assume a light or sound establishes a conversation with rescuers or a known arrival time.

For land deployment, AMSA calls for open space with the antenna upright toward the sky, clear of obstructions such as trees, structures and vehicles. Learn how your unit must be positioned and secured. This checklist does not instruct you to climb or enter danger to improve a signal. Water deployment differs; do not apply this land summary as a boating procedure.

Discuss separation before the trip. AMSA's current group guidance limits activation to two beacons within one group; separated groups should each activate a beacon. If individuals drift apart, personal beacons should be activated. Keep that distinction in the group's plan rather than assuming one unit will cover people who are no longer together.

Genuine distress: keep the beacon operating

Once activated for genuine distress, leave the beacon operating until rescue authorities tell you otherwise. Seeing or hearing a search aircraft is not that instruction. Weather, terrain, access and remoteness affect how long rescuers take to reach you. Plan for survival while waiting; this checklist gives no guaranteed rescue time.

Accidental activation: cancel the false alert

An accidental activation has a different response: switch the beacon off promptly and tell AMSA on 1800 641 792. Have the beacon identifier and incident details ready. AMSA states there is no penalty for an accidental activation. This number concerns the beacon alert; for an immediate life-threatening emergency use the emergency calling arrangements below.

After any incident, record the servicing/registration actions needed before the next journey. A completed checklist is not proof that a device, communications plan or trip is safe.
