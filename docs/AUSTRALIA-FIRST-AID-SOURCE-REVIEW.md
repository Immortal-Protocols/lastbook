Checked 26 November 2026. This records source selection and unresolved review work;
it is not a first-aid protocol, clinical approval or a grant of reuse rights.
The public collection remains 108 records. No candidate clinical material from
this review is added to the library or release assets.

These are documented source-selection decisions, not legal advice. A conditional

## Clinical wording requires a separate decision

The [Call, Push, Shock campaign page](https://www.ambulance.vic.gov.au/call-push-shock)
(updated 30 September 2026) and its [linked transcript](https://www.ambulance.vic.gov.au/media/988417)
(updated 22 November 2024) differ in recognition wording: the campaign introduction
joins its two conditions with “or”, while the transcript uses “and”. The transcript
also presents hands-only CPR without an explicit age/situation boundary. Its
source date must not be represented as a September 2026 clinical review.

ANZCOR Guideline 8 distinguishes compressions from the role of breaths by trained,
willing rescuers. A qualified reviewer should assess whether the campaign text
needs an explicit audience/situation boundary before it is presented alongside
child CPR, drowning or other first-aid material. This contribution does not merge
these sources into a newly authored treatment algorithm or declare an agency
source wrong. Reading a source is not independent clinical review.

The privately held Guideline 8 copy identifies approval in April 2021, a generated
information timestamp in September 2026, and acquisition in November 2026. Those
are different dates. A download/render timestamp is not evidence of a new clinical
review. The public audit contains metadata and original findings, not the private
guideline text or its extracted search units.

## Permission and clinical review packet

ANZCOR directs Australian enquiries to the Australian Resuscitation Council.
The [ARC contact page](https://www.resus.org.au/contact) lists info@resus.org.au,
rechecked 26 November 2026. A contributor enquiry is prepared locally; it has not
been sent and no permission or endorsement is claimed.

The enquiry asks about six existing bystander references: CPR, Australian snake
bite, spider bite, marine envenomation, pressure immobilisation and anaphylaxis.
It explicitly covers original files, indexing/text-only access, offline content
handling, source cards, public/workplace/commercial redistribution and required
clinical review. Personal-use permission is not a substitute for that response.

Before releasing a clinical collection, establish:

1. Exact permitted documents, versions, attribution, exclusions, redistribution and
   update terms, including any illustrations or third-party material.
2. Qualified review of audience/age/situation boundaries, the linked guideline set,
   sequence, recognition criteria and source variations. Keep approval, review,
   generation and retrieval dates separate.
3. Preservation of all relevant source conditions, diagrams, warnings and layout;
   a hash alone cannot prove clinical completeness or correct reading order.
4. A publication format consistent with the licence. An unchanged-PDF licence does
   not automatically authorize edited HTML, translations or a different protocol.
5. Verified offline search/citation/reader behavior, visible provenance and a clear
   refresh process. Neither stored references nor map points establish help arrival
   time, working communications or actual device availability.
