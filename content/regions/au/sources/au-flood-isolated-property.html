Rural property: prepare for flood isolation

Original scenario planning aid, 27 November 2026. Lastbook Australia contributors; CC BY 4.0: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/ . These planning prompts and blank worksheets are original. Linked official readings supply the factual guidance within their own dates, jurisdictions and conditions. They are not rewritten into a new technical or treatment protocol. No agency endorsement or independent professional assessment is claimed.

Plan for a rural property whose roads may close and whose electricity, water, communications and normal deliveries may fail together. Cover everyone living, working or visiting there, people needing assistance, pets and livestock. The isolation period must be established for this property with local information; it may exceed a week. Do not assume a seven-day household plan or the shorter stock periods in individual kit sources will be enough.

Isolation planning is not advice to stay in a flood-threatened property. Follow current local warnings and evacuation instructions. Plan to leave early while a safe route remains, particularly when essential care or safety cannot be maintained. Never drive, walk or swim through floodwater. Being cut off can prevent help, staff and supplies reaching you.

Before access closes: make decisions while you still have options

With council, the relevant emergency service and local land/animal services, establish the property's flood risks, likely isolation, warning sources and access constraints. Write down the evidence and date. Identify each access road, creek crossing, gate and alternate destination, who will check official status and the trigger for leaving before access is lost. Historical floods and static offline maps cannot prove a route is safe or open in this event.

Record who is on site, property address and coordinates, agreed contacts, accessible transport, drivers, animal transport and destination arrangements. Check that destinations can accommodate people, support needs, equipment and animals. Give accepted roles to a second person if the usual owner, worker or carer is away. Move animals early to safe higher ground with food, clean water and shelter, ensuring they cannot be trapped by rising water, as the NSW farm-flood source explains. Do not delay human departure to protect equipment.

Set the isolation horizon and a longer-isolation fallback

Property and people/animals present
Write the location, names or groups, responsible people, accessibility and care needs.
Planning duration and basis
Write the locally informed isolation assumption, uncertainty and review date; allow for simultaneous outage and interrupted deliveries.
Earliest access or safety decision
Write the warning/local-condition trigger, who decides and the safe action before a road, destination or essential backup is lost.
Longer-than-planned isolation
Write the next arrangement and how it will be activated before supplies, support or safe accommodation fail. Do not assume a resupply delivery or rescue will arrive on schedule.

Check the whole property, including dependent systems

For each row, record the daily requirement and basis, usable stock or verified backup, safe storage/access, responsible person, first failure point and earlier action. Multiply actual daily consumable needs by the chosen duration, include other uses and a contingency, then compare with usable stock. Keep human drinking water and livestock needs explicitly accounted for; do not substitute a household allowance for animal-specific advice.

Use the complete generator source and equipment instructions before relying on backup power. Never run a generator indoors or in an enclosed area or improvise a wall-socket connection; building connections require a licensed electrician. Check the real outdoor location, fuel supply and capacity. Read source-specific flood/electrical precautions. Do not operate, enter or reconnect damaged electrical equipment as part of this worksheet.

When a flood warning changes or isolation begins

Reassess people and animals, the current warning, access and the earliest essential limit. Tell the agreed off-property contact what has changed. Keep a dated log of who is present, safe supplies, verified backup remaining, support needs, animal welfare, warnings and the next check. Review at least daily and whenever conditions change; a change needing action must not wait until the next routine review.

If already isolated, do not attempt to cross floodwater to collect supplies or check stock. Contact the appropriate local emergency service about assistance and essential supply arrangements using a working service; record the contact and process before an event. Explain your exact location, people and animals affected, essential care needs and what is running short. A request is not confirmation of delivery or a safe way to remain. For immediate life-threatening danger, call 000. Confirm local SES/service responsibilities through the bundled jurisdiction directory rather than assuming a source from another state describes your service.

Agree check-in times, the communication method and what the outside contact will do after a missed contact or concern. Record the fallback if both sides lose their usual network. Mobile emergency calling needs coverage from an available network; an offline map, GPS fix or installed app does not itself create communications. Use the complete calling and accessible-calling readings.

Water, food and wastewater after inundation

Use the bundled water readings and actual local supply advice. Flood-affected water can be chemically as well as biologically contaminated; boiling does not remove chemical contamination. Do not infer safety from clear appearance. Read the complete food-safety source for flood contact, power failure and disposal conditions. Read NSW Health's complete on-site wastewater source and arrange advice/inspection for the actual system with council and a qualified service provider; this worksheet supplies no improvised tank repair or sewage-disposal method.

Recovery: water receding is not an all-clear

Check actual authority advice and road status before travel or return. Arrange professional assessment of damaged electrical equipment, pumps and wastewater systems. Account for everyone and inspect animals only when it can be done safely. Confirm safe water and food, accessible accommodation and the ability to sustain essential care. Record damage and assistance contacts, replenish used supplies, and review what failed with the household, workers and animal carers. Date the revised property plan.
