Accessible emergency calling: Australian service navigation

Original factual service-navigation note. Checked against ACMA and Access Hub on 26 November 2026. This page can be read offline; Lastbook cannot place a call or send an emergency message.

Choose the channel you use

  • Voice emergency calls: 000.
  • TTY emergency calls: 106. This requires a TTY/textphone. It is not an ordinary mobile SMS number.
  • NRS SMS Relay: 0423 677 767. The official emergency instructions say to include 000, the required service, your name, exact emergency location, brief details and GA (go ahead). Wait for the NRS confirmation and relay officer, answer the emergency officer's questions, and keep watching until the relay officer says the call is finished.
  • NRS Chat: nrschat.nrscall.gov.au. Use its Call 000 control for an emergency. It needs an internet connection.

Read the complete current instructions for your service

Access Hub explains SMS Relay, NRS Chat, NRS Captions, Voice Relay and the different TTY modes. Equipment, registration and connection requirements differ. Its emergency page advises using another NRS option instead of Video Relay because emergency Video Relay calls are not prioritised and the service has limited hours.

Complete NRS instructions: https://www.accesshub.gov.au/about-the-nrs/how-to-make-an-emergency-call-using-the-nrs. Service numbers: https://www.accesshub.gov.au/about-the-nrs/nrs-call-numbers-and-links. These websites and the relay services require a working connection; they are not included in this offline library. Check how your chosen service works before an emergency.

Read the bundled ACMA emergency-calls article for 000, 112, 106, coverage and text-message limits. Sending an SMS directly to 000 or 112 does not contact emergency services. SMS Relay is a separate channel.
