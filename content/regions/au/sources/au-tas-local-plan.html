Tasmania: access, warnings and distinct shelter arrangements

Original contributor planning reading, 26 November 2026.

Use this reading before an emergency to write and check your own arrangements. Lastbook cannot receive warnings, assess a building, confirm a route or open an evacuation centre.

Write down the local risk and the access you depend on

Tasmania SES describes planning around the people in a household, local hazards and interruptions to power, water, communications or road access. [T1] Start with your actual household or property: who is present at different times, who needs support, where people work or study, and which road or transport is needed to leave.

For a town household, check more than damage to the house: could the route to a carer, pharmacy or destination become unavailable? For a rural property, also map the gate, bridge or low section that could isolate the homestead, animals or workers. Ask the council about local flood exposure and access; do not infer it solely from how dry the house has stayed in earlier events. List the task, person and alternative for each critical dependency.

Keep evacuation destinations and bushfire last resorts distinct

Tasmania Fire Service identifies Nearby Safer Places as bushfire places of last resort. They are not evacuation centres or assembly areas. Facilities, entry to buildings, vehicle or animal access and emergency-service support cannot be assumed. Some communities have no identified site. [T3]

Give each option in your plan a clear purpose. First record a destination you have arranged for leaving early, with a person to confirm access and space. Separately record current official information about any bushfire place of last resort; it is not a replacement for the early-departure arrangement. A place assessed for a bushfire emergency does not establish protection from flood or another hazard. This reading does not select, rate or promise safe access to a site.

Check the destination with everyone who would use it. Ask about mobility and communication needs, essential equipment, medicines, transport, assistance animals and other animals. Record what the host or authority has actually confirmed and when. Do not assume pets, livestock or vehicles can accompany you to a Nearby Safer Place. For rural households, plan animal movements early enough that they do not depend on a late return through hazardous conditions.

Read map symbols with their limits

TasALERT distinguishes an evacuation-centre marker from a place-of-last-resort marker. A fire-incident point marks the reported starting location rather than its full spread. Its road-closure information is incomplete; the site also refers users to Tasmania Police. [T4]

When checking connected information, read the warning area, affected locations, issue time and requested action as well as the icon. An empty area on Lastbook's offline map does not mean that there is no incident, closure or hazard. A named hall, school or showground in the map is not evidence that it is open as a centre. Record where current opening and access instructions will come from and who will check them.

Make a warning and communication backup

SES directs people to TasALERT, ABC local radio and Bureau of Meteorology information. Its warning explanations pair Advice, Watch and Act, and Emergency Warning with actions for the affected location. [T2] Do not wait for a particular colour if you are already in danger or have been directed to act.

Choose someone who will check warnings and someone who can relay them if your connection fails. Write down an agreed check-in time and what a missed check-in means. Identify the radio frequency and power source you can actually receive at home and at an alternative destination. Practise using them. This reading does not supply live broadcasts, mobile coverage or a tested reception result.

Prepare for interrupted services and returning home

Try the plan against a road closure plus power failure. Which water pump, gate, charger, medical support or animal-care task stops first? Record a verified alternative, the person responsible, how long it works under actual use and when you must seek help or leave earlier. Use the isolation and dependency worksheet and the FSANZ emergency food reading for further planning.

SES's storm-preparation source includes property maintenance, an emergency kit, animals and health needs. [T5] Arrange specialist assessment for tasks beyond your capability, including roofs, trees, electricity and gas. Decide how you will obtain current permission and information before returning after an evacuation, and who can assess damage and animal needs. This reading does not certify a building, water supply, electrical system or animal enclosure for re-entry.
