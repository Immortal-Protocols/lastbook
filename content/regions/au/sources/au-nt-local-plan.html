Northern Territory: local warning, shelter and isolation worksheet

Original contributor planning aid, 26 November 2026. Use this with the people who share your home or property. Write answers on paper or in your own document; this page does not collect or save them. It connects national offline readings to decisions that need local NT information. It is not an NT Government emergency plan or an assessment of your property.

Build a local warning sheet before you lose connectivity

Use SecureNT for current NT warnings and the NT Fire Incident Map for bushfire incidents. Use Road Report NT for road information. These services are online; the map and readings saved in Lastbook do not receive their updates. Keep a dated record of the issuing authority, affected place and instruction. A fire danger forecast describes potential fire conditions; an incident warning concerns an actual event. Neither is a promise that your route will remain available.

Separate cyclone wind, storm surge, flood and bushfire decisions

SecureNT identifies storm surge risk for low-lying NT coastal areas. Surge can arrive before cyclone landfall. Check the official inundation information for your property and plan the destination and transport with the relevant authorities. Follow an instruction to evacuate. A wind-resistant room does not establish safety from rising water.

Home suitability and current instructions matter when deciding where to shelter from a cyclone. SecureNT says caravans are unsuitable cyclone shelters, and a temporary calm in the cyclone eye is not an all-clear. Obtain qualified building advice about an uncertain structure; this worksheet does not certify it. Do not apply a cyclone shelter decision to a bushfire or flood.

Start the hazard discussion with the saved national cyclone, flood and bushfire factsheet texts. Their general planning guidance does not select your NT destination or departure time. A mapped road does not establish access, permission or a safe evacuation route.

Confirm the actual shelter and animal arrangement

A listed NT public emergency shelter is not necessarily open. Wait for authorities to announce opening, then follow their instructions and leave before travel becomes dangerous. Record the exact facility, how opening will be confirmed and the arrangements needed by each person.

SecureNT distinguishes public emergency shelters from designated Darwin undercover car parks where people may remain in vehicles with restrained pets. Those car park options are not public emergency shelters; an ordinary car park is not a substitute. The shelter page notes limited toilets and services at car park options. Check the particular site's current designation, opening and restrictions. Arrange a separate plan for pets; confirm any assistance-animal access requirements directly rather than treating the pet rule as a determination of access.

Plan supplies for the actual isolation

SecureNT's kit page specifies supplies for at least 72 hours and at least 10 litres of bottled water per person for three days. This is one published household list, not a ceiling or a calculation of enough water for your climate, medical needs, animals or a longer disruption. Keep source quantities separate from the different lists elsewhere in the library.

Use the home and property isolation worksheet to identify what depends on mains power, pumps, deliveries or visiting support. For every dependency, write how long the available backup lasts under your actual use, how that was established, and the action needed before it runs out. Agree the plan with the relevant health, support, equipment or supply provider where their service is involved.

  • People and animals included, expected disruption and reason for that estimate: write your answer.
  • Water, food, medicines and other essentials actually available: record quantities, storage, expiry and access.
  • Pump, refrigeration, charging, communication and support dependencies: record the limiting dependency and tested backup.
  • Departure or help arrangements if a critical dependency cannot be maintained: agree and record them in advance.

Make the animal plan practical

SecureNT's animal guidance covers pets, horses and livestock separately. It calls for early relocation planning, reliable feed and water, identification and appropriate transport; it warns against moving animals after travel becomes unsafe or releasing them onto public roads. A household shelter plan alone does not solve livestock care.

Use NT-specific animal and property advice with a veterinarian, experienced handler and local authorities as appropriate. The NSW farm readings elsewhere remain NSW sources; they do not establish NT property duties. Keep a completed property map and access details private and share them only with the people you have chosen.
