Home and property isolation: dependency and backup worksheet

The prompts, organisation and blank arithmetic worksheets are contributor-authored. Cited agencies supply factual context; their manuals, artwork and branding are not reproduced or relicensed. No agency endorsement or independent professional review is implied.

Use this worksheet for a home or property that could lose road access, mains power, water or communications. It applies to everyday household needs as well as rural responsibilities. Copy it into your own plan and complete it with the people affected. Include the person's own priorities and agreed support arrangements.

1. Choose a disruption to plan for

Write a specific scenario, such as a road closed by flood combined with loss of power. Record the people, animals and essential activities affected; likely seasonal conditions; and which local authorities can clarify hazards and access. Use the relevant bundled hazard readings. A planning duration is an assumption to check, not a promise that roads or services will return by then.

2. Follow each dependency to its backup

Repeat the following record for every essential activity. Avoid relying on a backup that loses the same power, road or mobile connection as the main service.

Activity and person responsible
What must continue, who needs it, and who has agreed to help?
Dependencies
What does it require: electricity, a pump, refrigeration, a working device, safe water, accessible transport, a delivery or a support worker?
Failure sign
How will you notice it has stopped or is about to fail?
Verified backup
What alternative is actually available? Who confirmed its capacity, compatibility, accessibility and safe use, and when?
Duration and replenishment
How long can it meet this person's actual needs? How was that established? What happens if the supplier or usual helper cannot get through?
Action before the limit
Who acts, at which trigger, using which contact and transport? What is the fallback if the first arrangement fails?

Water, food and power

Check whether loss of electricity also stops water pumping, treatment, gates or refrigeration. Record safe drinking-water storage, food and ways to access them if equipment fails. Use the rainwater/groundwater and food-safety readings for their source-specific conditions. Appearance alone does not verify water safety. Write down what local water advice or testing is needed.

If a generator is part of the plan, read the bundled Energy Safe Victoria instructions before relying on it. Include fuel, safe operating location and the equipment it can actually supply. Confirm any connection to building wiring with a licensed electrician. This worksheet does not specify a generator size, wiring method or battery runtime.

Medicines, equipment and assistance

With the person and their care/equipment providers, record the agreed plan for prescriptions, storage requirements, equipment backup, communication access and assistance with daily activities. Get actual duration and safe testing instructions from the appropriate provider. Do not disconnect essential medical equipment merely to try this checklist. Record an agreed alternative if the usual support person, power source or delivery is unavailable. This is planning, not a change to treatment or medication.

Animals and property tasks

List water, feed, medicines, fencing/gates and safe access needed for pets or livestock. Identify who can carry out each task if you cannot. NSW's farm-flood source calls for early animal movement and supplies protected above expected flood levels; its specific quantities and responsibilities remain NSW source advice. Confirm local transport, holding arrangements and hazard information for your property rather than assuming every regional rule applies nationwide.

3. Plan for a missed contact

Record accessible warning sources, a battery-powered information option, charged communications and an alternative when mobile service fails. Agree who checks on whom, at what time and time zone, and what action follows a missed contact. Include the relevant emergency, utility, council, care-provider and trusted-person numbers in the completed plan. Check them before an event; this app does not deliver warnings or make calls.

4. Set the point for leaving early

Record the warning or local-condition trigger for reconsidering the plan, the person who decides, and the action required before a route or essential backup is lost. Agree a destination, accessible transport, a backup driver/support person, essential equipment and animal arrangements. Verify that the destination can meet the person's needs. Follow local authorities' instructions and the relevant hazard advice; do not use a supply calculation as a reason to delay evacuation or enter floodwater.

Write down what you will do if the preferred route or destination is unavailable. Include how to account for everyone, tell the agreed contacts and carry current documents and necessary equipment. Static map symbols do not establish an open evacuation centre or accessible road.

5. Check the plan together

Discuss one realistic failure with everyone involved. Confirm that helpers have accepted their role, essential items can be reached, communication methods are usable and backup durations are supported by actual information. Record unresolved questions, who will resolve them and the next review date. Update the plan after changes to health, household, stock, equipment, suppliers or access. Use the disability-inclusive source readings to support a plan made with the person.
