Western Australia: current warnings, departure and animal planning

Original contributor planning reading, 26 November 2026.

Use this reading before an emergency to write and check your own arrangements. Lastbook cannot receive warnings, assess a building, confirm a route or open an evacuation centre.

Start with the right warning system

Western Australia adopted the Australian Warning System for bushfire, cyclone, flood and storm on 15 July 2024. The current levels are Advice, Watch and Act, and Emergency Warning, accompanied by an action statement. The former cyclone Blue Alert, Yellow Alert, Red Alert and All Clear sequence is no longer the WA system. A lower warning level after an event does not establish that all hazards have gone. Read the actual location and action in a current warning; do not translate an old colour chart into a new instruction. [W1]

Write down your property or accommodation location and the places you travel through. Decide who checks current Emergency WA information, who can relay it if your usual connection fails, and how you will recognise that your last information is stale. Check whether an older printed plan still assumes the retired cyclone colours. A weather forecast, fire danger rating and incident warning answer different questions.

Separate wind, water and departure decisions

DFES identifies coastal surge, inland flooding and damaging wind among cyclone impacts. A cyclone weakening does not remove those hazards. [W2] Make a separate entry in your plan for each relevant hazard: the place affected, how you would learn of a change, the action you would take, the person responsible and the latest safe point for completing it.

For your household, verify a destination and transport for everyone who may be present, including visitors and people who need assistance. For a farm or remote property, also check staff, contractors and anyone working away from the homestead. Record who has keys, who can operate necessary equipment and what happens if that person is absent. Have building suitability assessed by an appropriate local professional; a roof type or map label alone cannot settle cyclone or flood safety.

Before travelling, ask your accommodation or worksite how it handles a cyclone and an early departure. Record how long packing, loading and the journey actually take. Confirm the route through current Main Roads information and the relevant local road authority. Main Roads provides a travel map for roadworks and closures; this offline reading contains no road-status feed. [W5] An unmarked road on an old map is not evidence that it is passable.

Keep the animal plan separate from human accommodation

DPIRD distinguishes companion animals, horses and livestock, and asks owners to plan for their care and identification. It says ordinary animals cannot be assumed entry inside community evacuation centres; recognised assistance animals are a separate exception. Local government and centre staff can advise on arrangements. [W3]

For each animal group, record a confirmed receiving place, the transport and handler needed, and a backup if that place cannot receive them. Check whether a float, crate or truck can load the entire group in time; include animals agisted elsewhere. Agree with the other owner or carer who will act. Ask about access, containment, shade, feed, water and any veterinary needs at the receiving site. Do not treat a showground symbol or a human evacuation-centre marker as an animal booking.

For animals that cannot be moved, arrange property-specific advice in advance. Record how feed, water and containment depend on power, pumps, fencing, staff or road deliveries. Identify what can fail together and who could safely check it. These questions do not designate a paddock or building as safe, authorise entry through a closure or replace veterinary and local emergency advice.

Test the plan before conditions deteriorate

Run through a loss of mobile service, a blocked access road and an unavailable driver. Use the actual time needed to contact helpers, gather medicines and equipment, load people and animals, and reach the agreed place. If the plan only works with one phone, one person or one route, identify a workable alternative or seek help to change it. Keep a paper copy where everyone responsible can find it.

Use the household and property isolation worksheet to work through supply dependencies. Use the remote journey worksheet for travel arrangements. Neither worksheet establishes a safe route or sufficient supplies for your circumstances.
