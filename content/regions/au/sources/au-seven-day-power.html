Household: seven days without mains power

Original scenario planning aid, 27 November 2026. Lastbook Australia contributors; CC BY 4.0: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/ . These planning prompts and blank worksheets are original. Linked official readings supply the factual guidance within their own dates, jurisdictions and conditions. They are not rewritten into a new technical or treatment protocol. No agency endorsement or independent professional assessment is claimed.

Plan for seven consecutive days without mains electricity. Seven days is this exercise's chosen horizon, not an official prediction, a safe waiting period or a requirement to remain home. Also ask what happens if the outage lasts longer, water stops or someone cannot get to you. Make the plan with everyone affected, including children, people with disability, carers and neighbours who have agreed to help. Keep an accessible paper copy and the relevant readings where they can be used without this computer.

Before the outage: establish what must keep working

Write the address, people and pets present, start/end assumptions, seasonal heat or cold, and who is responsible. Identify electrical dependencies beyond lights: medical and assistive equipment, medicine storage, water pumps and treatment, refrigeration, cooking, heating/cooling, lifts, powered doors, gates, alarms, communications and charging. In an apartment, ask building management about common doors, lifts, water pumps and emergency arrangements before relying on them.

With the person and their care, pharmacy and equipment providers, confirm the actual arrangements for essential care, prescriptions, storage, device compatibility, backup duration and a suitable alternative location. Record agreed help if a support worker or delivery cannot arrive. Do not change treatment, ration medicines or disconnect essential medical equipment to test this plan. An unverified backup for an essential need is a gap to resolve before the event.

Cover all seven days, task by task

Copy a row for each essential task. Record units consistently. For consumable supplies only, estimate the amount needed each day, add the seven daily amounts, then compare with usable stock actually accessible during the outage. Include guests, pets, preparation and cleaning where relevant. Write down how the estimate was established and a contingency if needs increase. A number of days printed in a short emergency-kit source does not establish seven-day adequacy for your household.

A battery's label or a generator's fuel-tank size alone does not verify this schedule. Record equipment-specific evidence, who checked it and when. This worksheet supplies no generator sizing, wiring instructions, fuel-storage allowance or medical battery-runtime calculation. Read the complete generator source and the manufacturer's instructions; use a licensed electrician for building connections. Never operate a generator indoors or in an enclosed area, or improvise a connection through a wall socket. Confirm a safe outdoor location for the actual equipment and surroundings before relying on it.

At the start: protect people and record the time

Note when power failed. Check everyone's immediate needs and activate the agreed care and contact plan. Use torches or battery lighting. Check official warnings and your electricity distributor's information through an available means; record its fault contact in advance. Treat any restoration estimate as uncertain. Use the food-safety readings promptly: keep fridge/freezer doors closed as much as possible, follow the complete source conditions, and do not taste food to decide whether it is safe. Their approximate cold-storage times are not seven-day storage promises.

If water pumping or treatment stops, use the safe supply already planned. Read the rainwater, non-potable-water and boil-water-alert sources for their different conditions. Boiling does not remove chemical contamination. A dated source is not an actual supplier alert or confirmation that your supply is safe. Include sanitation and hand hygiene; ask the relevant provider/council about a failed wastewater system.

Review every day, and whenever conditions change

Complete this log on paper. Check people, care, food safety, safe water, sanitation, power/fuel remaining, temperature, official information and agreed contacts. Compare the remaining verified capacity with the remaining need. Act before the earliest essential limit; do not wait for the daily review if someone becomes unwell, a warning changes or a backup fails.

Leave or seek help before an essential limit is reached

Agree a decision point for each essential need, a destination able to meet it, accessible transport and a fallback if that destination or helper is unavailable. Include extreme heat/cold and inability to obtain safe water, maintain required care or use essential equipment. Follow actual authority instructions; supplies are not a reason to delay evacuation. In a life-threatening emergency call 000 using a working service. Most NBN-connected services need power; mobile emergency calls still require coverage from an available mobile network. Read the emergency-calling and accessible-calling sources and agree an alternative if the usual phone or network fails.

Day seven is a review point, not an automatic finish

If power is still off, activate the longer-outage arrangement before remaining capacity fails. When power returns, follow the distributor's instructions and obtain professional checks for damaged or flood-affected equipment. Recheck food and water using the relevant source conditions rather than assuming restoration makes them safe. Refill used supplies, review failed assumptions with everyone involved and date the revised plan.
