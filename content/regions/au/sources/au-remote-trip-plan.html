Remote land journey: route, supplies and check-in worksheet

Use this worksheet before a remote land journey. Copy the prompts onto paper or into your own document; this reading has no fill-in fields. Agree on the answers with everyone travelling and the person holding the plan. Keep a usable copy without internet access. Review it when the route, people, vehicle or conditions change.

1. Describe the actual journey

  • Plan version, preparation date and time zone:
  • Travellers, relevant support needs, vehicle description and registration:
  • Departure, intended return, route and overnight stops:
  • Who holds the plan, their backup, and how to tell them about changes:

Record each leg separately: start and destination; distance; expected driving and rest time; track restrictions and permits; planned stops; alternatives; and the last safe decision point for turning back. Ask the relevant road manager, park or local authority about current access. Record who supplied the information and when. A line or named place on an offline map does not establish a public, passable road, an open fuel outlet or safe drinking water.

2. Account for supplies leg by leg

myGov advises researching the area, arranging fuel/rest stops and carrying sufficient food and water. Queensland's travel checklist also includes spare supplies and vehicle preparation. Use the bundled source readings below alongside route-specific advice. Resolve an unknown essential resupply point before leaving; do not count it as confirmed supply.

Fuel worksheet

For each leg, record distance in kilometres, the consumption figure chosen for the actual vehicle/load/terrain, usable fuel carried, confirmed resupply, and a separate allowance for delays, idling, detours and reserve. Write down how each figure was established and who checked it.

Basic arithmetic only: estimated driving fuel in litres = distance in kilometres × chosen consumption in litres per 100 kilometres ÷ 100. This is before additional allowances. A normal-road figure may not describe sand, low-range driving, towing or a loaded vehicle. Tank capacity alone does not establish a safe range. This worksheet supplies no universal consumption, reserve or safe travel distance.

Water and other essentials worksheet

Record the number of people, the period you must cover including a planned delay, the drinking-water allowance chosen for these conditions, and how it was checked. Basic arithmetic for drinking water alone is people × chosen litres per person per day × days covered. Separately account for cooking, hygiene, animals, medical needs and reserve. Heat, exertion and individual needs require suitable advice; the worksheet supplies no universal daily minimum. Record which carried containers hold safe drinking water and which resupply has actually been confirmed. Do not assume boiling makes chemical contamination safe.

List food, medicines and first-aid supplies, hot/cold-weather clothing, vehicle repair equipment you can use, and how those items will remain accessible during a breakdown. Seek experienced local/vehicle advice for the route and load. Use the actual device and vehicle instructions rather than guessing carrying limits or repair procedures.

3. Agree on communications and overdue action

For each leg, record the working communication method, limitations, power supply and backup. A mobile emergency call still needs coverage from a suitable network; an offline map cannot provide it. Keep the emergency calling and beacon readings available. A beacon is distinct from a two-way conversation.

  • Check-in time and time zone, method, recipient and backup recipient:
  • What the recipient should do if a check-in is missed, using which contact details and information:
  • Who will notify the plan holder before a route or return-time change:
  • What to do when the planned method has no service:

Concern comes before a timetable. Tasmania Police states that reporting someone missing does not require a 24-hour wait. If their location is unknown and you fear for their welfare, contact police promptly. An agreed check-in deadline is not a reason to delay urgent help. Use 000 for an immediate life-threatening emergency. Find the relevant police service through the Australian directory; Tasmania's intake arrangements are not presented here as a universal national procedure.

With the travellers' agreement, give the trusted plan holder the route, last known position/time, vehicle details, names and useful identification, relevant assistance needs and communication details. Agree who will pass this information to police. Share sensitive health or personal information only as appropriate for the plan; this app neither stores your completed worksheet nor lodges an itinerary.

4. Rehearse a changed plan

Discuss an inaccessible track, closed fuel stop, lost communications and breakdown. Write the trigger, action and responsible person for each. Queensland Parks' remote-park guidance advises remaining with a broken-down vehicle because it is easier to locate than a person. That does not mean remaining in immediate danger from traffic, fire or rising water. Obtain advice for the actual setting and keep the group accounted for. Do not turn an unverified shortcut or walk for help into the default backup.

Before departure, confirm that the plan holder has the latest version, necessary communications work as intended, essential supplies are accounted for, and everyone knows the decision points. This is a preparation worksheet, not training in remote driving, recovery, navigation or first aid.
